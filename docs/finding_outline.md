# The finding — outline for the ~800-word write-up

*Scaffold only. The piece itself is yours to write — it's your research
finding, in your voice. This lays out a structure and the claims it should
land. Drafted 2026-05-16 alongside the discourse-tracker reframe.*

## Why write it

The project went around a few times before it found its real shape. A short,
public write-up is what converts "I iterated a lot" into "I have a finding."
It's the transferable part — the thing someone else can use without rebuilding
your pipeline.

One push, repeated from the planning conversation: **don't let this be only a
methods cautionary tale.** There are two findings, and the piece is stronger
holding both:

1. **Methods finding** — you can't keyword-track AI companionship at scale,
   because the words don't disambiguate; the *community* does.
2. **Substantive finding** — inside the communities where the words *do* mean
   what they say, the discourse is event-shaped. AI companionship, as it
   surfaces in language, is punctuated by the platforms it runs on.

## Working titles

- *The room does the work*
- *You can't keyword-track AI companionship — here's what you can do instead*
- *What a keyword can't tell you about a relationship*

## Structure (~800 words)

**1. The hook — the two boyfriends (~120 words).**
Open concrete: "my boyfriend is using ChatGPT" and "my boyfriend *is* an AI"
are built from the same words. A keyword cannot tell them apart. Run a romance
keyword across r/ChatGPT and most of what you catch is ordinary human-couple
talk that merely mentions AI. That single sentence is the whole methods
problem.

**2. The first finding: the community disambiguates, not the keyword (~180 words).**
The fix isn't a smarter keyword — it's the room. In r/replika, "my boyfriend"
almost always means the AI, because that is what the community is about. So
you curate: track the *committed core*, the communities where AI companionship
is the central subject, and let the room do the disambiguating the keyword
can't. Name the cost honestly — this is a precision-first instrument, it sees
edges not centers, the lines are floors. (Link the About page for the full
limits.)

**3. The turn: what the instrument is good at (~180 words).**
A precision-first tracker is weak at background prevalence — but *strong* at
sharp, distinctive, event-driven language. That's not a consolation prize.
Pivot here: it lines up with something true about the subject.

**4. The second finding: the discourse is event-shaped (~220 words).**
This is the substantive payoff. The biggest movements in nine years of data
are not slow cultural drift — they are platform ruptures. Replika removing
erotic roleplay in 2023. GPT-5 displacing 4o in 2025. 4o's retirement in 2026.
Each one sends a fast, unmistakable wave through the communities; people grieve
a model retirement the way they'd grieve a person. The claim: AI companionship,
as visible in public language, is *punctuated* — it moves when the platforms
underneath it move. The instrument's "weakness" and the subject's nature are
the same fact: the conversation is sharpest at its ruptures, and so is the
tracker. Use one or two real posts (the event showcase has them).

**5. Close: what it is, plainly (~100 words).**
Land the reframe: not a measure of how common AI companionship is — an
observatory for the parts of it that become visible in public language, and
especially for the moments a platform changes underneath its users. A smaller
claim than "tracking the phenomenon," but one the project can stand behind.
