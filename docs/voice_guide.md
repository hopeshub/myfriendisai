# Voice & Copy Guide — My Friend Is AI

*Created 2026-05-15. The standard for the site-wide copy review and for any
copy written or edited afterward.*

This is not an invented brand voice. The site already has one — plain,
honest, editorial, calm. This guide names it so a copy pass sharpens it
instead of drifting it.

---

## 1. Who's reading, and what the site is

**The reader** is a curious non-expert — someone "somewhat interested" in
whether AI-companionship trends are real and roughly what's going on. Not a
researcher. They will read a headline, glance at the charts, maybe click into
one theme. Write for them.

**The site** is an independent, one-person research project. Honest,
clearly-sourced, genuinely useful — and *not* peer-reviewed scholarship. It
should never posture as more rigorous than it is. It should also look polished
and load fast; sloppy copy reads as sloppy data.

**Every piece of copy has a job.** A headline orients. The methodology note
protects credibility. A theme story explains a shape. Before editing a
sentence, know what job it does — then judge it on that, not on prettiness.

---

## 2. The voice — six principles

**1. Plain, but precise.** Write for a smart non-expert: short words, full
sentences, no jargon you don't need. When a technical term is unavoidable,
explain it in place (the "detector" chip carries a hover explanation).
Precision does not require jargon — *"It measures one thing: how the language
of AI companionship shows up in public Reddit posts over time."*

**2. Honest before impressive.** The site's entire credibility is that it
tells you what it cannot show. State limits plainly and early — *"not how many
people are in relationships with AI, and not a peer-reviewed academic study."*
No sentence may imply more certainty or more rigor than the data supports.

**3. Hedge only where it's load-bearing — then hedge exactly.** A caveat must
do real work; if it doesn't change what the reader should believe, cut it.
When you do hedge, be specific: *"the shape and timing are approximately
honest"* beats *"results may vary."* A pile of vague hedges is as dishonest as
too few — it buries the ones that matter.

**4. Concrete over abstract.** Name the event, the date, the number, the
community. *"In February 2023 Replika removed erotic roleplay"* — not *"a
platform policy change."* Specifics are both the texture and the evidence.

**5. Calm, not hyped.** This is a research artifact, not a launch. No
"explosive," no "skyrocketing," no exclamation marks. Let the chart and the
specifics carry the weight; confidence comes from precision, not adjectives.

**6. Every sentence earns its place.** Cut throat-clearing, restated caveats,
and hedging about hedging. If a sentence doesn't change what the reader knows
or does, delete it. (This project has a documented history of over-explaining
— guard against it actively.)

---

## 3. House style

**Terminology — one term per concept:**
- **community** in prose; `r/Name` or "subreddit" only when being literal
  about Reddit mechanics.
- **theme** — never "topic" or "category" in reader-facing copy.
- **validated-keyword mention** / **mention rate** — hyphenate consistently;
  don't drift to "hit rate." ("hits" / "tags" stay in code and dev docs only.)
- **AI companion(s)** for the thing; **AI companionship** for the phenomenon.
- Site name is **My Friend Is AI** (no other casing).

**Numbers.** Approximate values take `~` *or* "roughly"/"about" — not both.
Ranges use an en-dash (`3–32%`). State the unit on first use. **Any number
that appears on more than one page must match** — there is one true value;
copy that restates it can go stale. (Flag mismatches during the review.)

**Punctuation.** The em-dash is a signature here — used for asides and pivots.
Keep it, but no more than one per sentence. Curly quotes and apostrophes
throughout. Quote example keywords/phrases in curly quotes: *"therapeutic."*

**Headings.** Sentence case, no terminal period — *"Why mention rates don't
compare."*

**No exclamation marks.** No emoji in prose (theme emoji are UI icons, fine).

**Bold** is reserved for the single load-bearing claim of a section — *"The
chart shows a floor, not a ceiling."* Don't scatter it.

---

## 4. Do / Don't

| Don't | Do |
|---|---|
| "AI companionship is exploding across Reddit." | "Reddit communities for AI companionship have grown steadily since 2023." |
| "Results may not be fully accurate." | "The chart shows a floor — it undercounts theme-relevant discussion by a wide, theme-specific margin." |
| "We use a sophisticated keyword-validation methodology." | "Every keyword is checked by hand against 100 real posts before it's used." |
| "This important and rigorous research demonstrates…" | (Cut it. Show the work; let it stand.) |

---

## 5. The one rule for the review itself

Separate **style edits** from **claims edits**.

- *Style* — clarity, rhythm, word choice, cutting: make freely.
- *Claims* — anything that changes what is **claimed or measured**: a
  precision figure, the strength of a hedge, a scope limit, a number. **Flag
  these; decide them deliberately.** Never let a prose pass quietly strengthen
  a hedge into an overclaim or drop a load-bearing caveat. The honesty
  calibration here was hard-won.

---

## 6. Voice: first person, and don't disclaim status

**Write in the first person — "I".** It's a one-person project, and "I track
six themes…" reads like a person; the editorial "we" reads like an institution
the project isn't. Keep it consistent across every page.

**Don't disclaim the project's status.** No "independent project, no
institution, no funding" — and no academic posturing either. It is neither a
lab nor something to apologize for being small. Just talk about the work; let
what it is come through in how plainly and confidently it's described.
